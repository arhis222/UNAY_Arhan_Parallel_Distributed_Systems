CAUSALLY-ORDERED MULTICAST - SPECIFICATION
Arhan UNAY

Based on L3-Multicast, slides 27-40.

1. Purpose
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each member can send a message to the entire group, including itself. If a
multicast causally precedes another multicast, members must deliver them in
that order. For example, a reply must not be delivered before the post that
caused it. The library uses message dependencies, not the meaning of the text.

Consecutive sends by one member are ordered. Delivering a message before
sending another also creates a dependency. These dependencies are transitive.
Concurrent messages may be delivered in different orders at different members.
This is the difference from total order.

2. Guarantees and assumptions
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
I will use a static group, known member identities and reliable FIFO channels.
No failures are assumed in the first version. Every multicast is eventually
delivered once at every member; messages are not invented or altered.
Reception can happen before delivery when dependencies are still missing.

The API will use multicast(String) and a delivered(String) listener, like the
TOM interface, with a different ordering guarantee.
The listener must be installed before starting. Applications must display
their own messages through delivery too.

3. Failure and restart extension
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Following slide 40, I will also consider fail-stop crashes and restart on the
same machine. The extension will use reliable broadcast and retained history.
At least one correct member must retain the required history; a complete
loss of the group state is outside this design.

Missing dependencies must be recovered, not skipped when their sender dies.
A restarted member must recover its vector and application state before
sending new messages. Replaying history is not a guarantee that external
application effects execute exactly once.
